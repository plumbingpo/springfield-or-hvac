Signs Your Air Conditioner Is Failing in Springfield, OR

Home / Blog / Signs Your Air Conditioner Is Failing in Springfield, OR

As the warmest months approach here in Springfield, OR, our air conditioners become hard-working systems of our home comfort. These units operate constantly to keep our indoor spaces cool and comfortable. But like any mechanical system, air conditioners will eventually wear down and will eventually begin to show signs of wear and tear, indicating that a costly failure might be on the horizon. Being aware of these symptoms can save you from a sudden and uncomfortable breakdown on the hottest day of the year, and potentially reduce repair costs in the long run. Here at Springfield HVAC, we want to help you understand what to look out for.

Reduced Cooling Performance: Feeling Less Cool Than Before?

One of the most common and immediate signs that your air conditioner is struggling is a drop in its cooling capability. You could be setting the thermostat to colder temperatures only to still feel warm or humid indoors. Uneven cooling throughout your house is a tell-tale sign. This isn't just about comfort; it indicates a problem within the system. There are multiple potential causes for this problem. It could be a depleted refrigerant charge, which means your system isn't able to absorb and release heat effectively. Refrigerant is the lifeblood of your AC. Another common culprit is a dirty air filter, which restricts airflow and makes the unit work harder without achieving the desired cooling. If the evaporator coils are fouled, cooling suffers, as they cannot efficiently transfer heat. The compressor itself, the engine of your AC, could be giving out. If you're experiencing inadequate cooling, it's time to have a professional from Springfield HVAC diagnose the problem.

Strange Noises: Listening to Your AC for Clues

The sound of a properly functioning AC is usually a quiet, steady whir. If you start hearing unexpected auditory disturbances coming from your unit, it's a strong signal that something is amiss. Grinding or squealing noises often point to issues with the motor bearings. These bearings, key to rotational movement, can wear down over time, leading to friction and eventual motor failure if not addressed. Clicking sounds, especially persistent ones, could indicate a failing relay or a problem with the electrical components. Loud banging or clunking is especially worrisome, as they might suggest a component that has come dislodged and is striking other parts. Don't delay in addressing these auditory warnings. Heeding these sound warnings can prevent minor issues from becoming major expenses.

Increased Energy Bills: The Hidden Cost of a Failing AC

A subtle but significant sign of a struggling AC is a gradual rise in your electricity costs. If you haven't changed your usage habits but your monthly utility costs are suddenly much higher, your AC unit might be working harder than it should to achieve the same level of cooling. Several issues can lead to this lack of efficiency. As mentioned, a dirty filter reduces airflow, forcing the fan motor to work overtime. If your ducts are leaking, you're cooling your attic instead of your home, meaning your AC has to run longer to maintain the desired temperature. Units that are too old or not properly sized for your home are inherently less efficient. Refrigerant leaks also force the system to work harder. Over time, components like the compressor and fan motors can lose their efficiency. If you're noticing a significant uptick in your energy expenses without a clear explanation, it's worth having Springfield HVAC inspect your AC unit to identify the source of the inefficiency. Our team can help you determine the most cost-effective solution.

Poor Airflow: Weak Air from Your Vents?

Strong airflow is essential for both comfort and system efficiency. If you notice that the air coming out of your vents is weak, or if some rooms have significantly less airflow than others, it's a major sign of an underlying problem. This problem can stem from various causes. The most frequent cause is a heavily soiled or obstructed air filter, which restricts the volume of air that can pass through your system. If return vents are blocked, airflow will suffer. Beyond filters, issues with the fan motor itself can lead to reduced airflow. A struggling fan motor won't adequately circulate air through your home's ducts. Obstructions in the ductwork, such as debris or even collapsed sections, can also be a factor. Closed dampers in your duct system can lead to inconsistent airflow distribution. Addressing poor airflow is important not just for comfort, but also because it forces your AC to work harder, potentially shortening its lifespan.

Excessive Moisture or Leaks: Unwanted Wetness Near Your System?

While a small amount of condensation is normal during AC operation, excessive moisture or actual puddles around your indoor unit are not. This is a unmistakable indicator that something is wrong. A blocked condensate drain is the leading cause of water leaks. Humidity removed from the air collects in a drain pan and exits via a condensate line during cooling. A clogged condensate line will cause water to back up and spill. This can result in damage to your property and even pose electrical risks. Beyond a clogged drain, a cracked or rusted drain pan can also cause leaks. A significant refrigerant leak can lead to ice forming on the evaporator coils, which then melts and overfills the pan. Any standing water or excessive moisture near your AC, it's important to call for service right away to prevent further damage. Springfield HVAC can diagnose the source of the leak and provide a lasting solution.

Frequent Cycling: Is Your AC Turning On and Off Too Often?

An AC unit should operate in steady cycles, cooling your home to the set temperature before turning off. If your AC starts turning on and off rapidly, a phenomenon called "short cycling," it's a serious indicator of a problem. When an AC short cycles, it fails to finish a complete cooling cycle, leading to inefficiency and system strain. There are various potential causes for this behavior. If your AC is too big for your home, it might short cycle, cooling fast but not dehumidifying. Often, short cycling is due to low refrigerant, a dirty filter, or a faulty thermostat. A failing compressor or an electrical issue can also lead to short cycling. Whatever the reason, frequent short cycling accelerates wear on AC components, resulting in earlier breakdowns and higher energy use. If your AC is short cycling, it's not operating efficiently or effectively, and it needs professional attention.

Old Age: How Old Is Your AC Unit?

Even without obvious problems, your AC's age is a key indicator of its approaching end-of-life. A central AC system generally has a lifespan of 10 to 15 years, though some last longer with diligent care. An AC unit nearing or past its expected lifespan will be more prone to issues and less efficient. Older units often use outdated refrigerants like R-22, which are no longer manufactured and are becoming increasingly expensive to service due to supply limitations. Years of operation mean components in older systems are simply more fatigued. While maintenance prolongs life, there comes a time when repairs outweigh the cost of a new, efficient system. If your AC is over a decade old and showing symptoms, it's wise to consider replacement options. A new, energy-efficient model can offer significant savings on your utility bills and provide superior comfort for years to come.
